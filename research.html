MOF/Polymer composites for VOCs capture

My core PhD work models composites of metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) with hydrophobic polymers .

Rather than treating the polymer as a surface coating, I study chains that physically infiltrate the MOF channels and restructure the pore network. The two systems behave very differently: DUT-4/PVDF shows a bimodal pore-size distribution with a red-shifted primary peak, consistent with deep polymer infiltration and confinement-driven chain extension, while DUT-5/PVDF shows an attenuated but unshifted main peak with additional features from bulk polymer free volume, constricted entrance apertures, and interfacial voids. Toluene residence time at the MOF entrance is roughly four times longer in DUT-5/PVDF, with an order-of-magnitude difference in self-diffusivity in the MOF region.

Selected directions

Multi-scale pipeline

From electrons to pore networks

An end-to-end workflow spanning DFT, GCMC, all-atom MD, and graph-theoretic pore-network analysis — including accessible free-volume profiling and void-grid community detection to quantify how infiltration reshapes the pore topology.

Machine learning for materials

PoreGraphNet

A graph-neural-network framework (with Bayesian active learning) for predicting MOF/polymer composite properties, built to represent infiltration-modified pore networks — a regime existing models do not capture. Design space spans multiple MOFs, polymers, and target VOCs.

Foundations

First-principles materials modelling

Earlier DFT work on 2D transition-metal dichalcogenides, perovskites, and spinels, including Mn-doped MoS₂ for electrocatalysis — the foundation I now build on for porous-materials simulation.
