About

I am a computational materials scientist working across scales — DFT, grand-canonical Monte Carlo, molecular dynamics, and graph-theoretic pore-network analysis — to understand and design materials that capture volatile organic compounds (VOCs).

My PhD focuses on composites of metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) and hydrophobic polymers. The part I find most interesting, and that sets the work apart, is studying polymer chains that physically infiltrate the MOF channels and restructure the 3D pore topology — rather than simply coating the surface. This is a regime current machine-learning models do not capture, and it changes how a material transports and traps guest molecules.

Before my PhD I trained as a physicist (MSc), applying DFT to 2D transition-metal dichalcogenides, perovskites, and spinels, before moving into all-atom MD and GCMC for porous materials. I build my analysis pipelines in Python (MDTraj, NetworkX, pymatgen) on top of LAMMPS and RASPA simulations.
